Hey friend,

Let’s be real, if you’ve ever had a dog lose their mind the second the doorbell rings, you know exactly what chaos looks like.

My dog Bruce used to host his own welcome parade: barking, jumping, and charging the door like the guest owed him rent. It got to the point where I was low-key hoping people wouldn’t stop by.

I used to dread visitors because of the drama until I stumbled on a genius little system I now call the “Visitor Protocol.” And let me tell you it changed everything. If your pet parenting style leans “Constable” (you know, rules, routines, a little law and order), this might just become your new favorite trick.

Here’s the magic:

Before opening the door, I say one word, “Place.”

That’s Bruce’s cue to go to his designated “calm zone” a dog bed a few feet away from the entrance. He has to sit and stay before I even touch the door handle.

Once the guest is inside and Bruce’s tail isn’t spinning like a propeller anymore, he gets the green light to come say hi—calmly.

No jumping, no licking people’s faces, and definitely no sniffing where he shouldn’t.

How to train it:

  1. Pick a calm zone: A bed, mat, or towel near the entryway but not right at the door.

  2. Teach the command: I use “Place,” but “Mat” or “Zone” works too. Lure them onto the spot with a treat, say the word, and reward. Rinse and repeat.

  3. Practice before guests arrive: Do dry runs with family or friends. Fake door knocks, real praise.

  4. Be consistent: Every visitor. Every time. No exceptions.

  5. Make it rewarding: Once your pet stays in place until released, give them a treat or a toy to say “nice work.”

Why it works:

This taps into your pet’s need for structure. Dogs (and yes, even cats!) thrive when they know what’s expected of them. As Constable-style pet parents, we love a routine, and this is like a traffic light system for behavior. It keeps everyone calm, and guests won’t leave with fur-covered pants or a nose-to-knee greeting.

And honestly? It made Bruce feel more confident. He doesn’t get overstimulated at the door anymore, and the house doesn’t erupt into barking madness every time someone walks by.

The best part? You can try this tonight.

Grab a mat, pick a word, and start small. You’ll be shocked at how quickly they catch on.

It’s fun, it’s empowering, and it makes you feel like you’re running a smooth operation instead of a circus.
