Why People Don’t See Returns on Their Investments

You remember that feeling and the quiet mix of excitement and anxiety when you finally decided to “start investing.”

Maybe it began with a friend’s advice:

“Don’t just save. Make your money work for you.”

You opened your first investment account, either in mutual funds, crypto, or real estate crowdfunding. You read the posts, followed the right pages, and waited because that’s what smart people do, right? They invest.

Weeks turned into months. You checked your balance, expecting progress, but it looked the same.

A few more months passed. Still nothing.

Soon, excitement turned into confusion.

You began to wonder: Am I doing something wrong?

Is the market just bad? Why is everyone else talking about profit while mine seems stuck?

You’re not alone.

Across Africa and beyond, thousands of people, especially first-time investors face the same frustration. They take the bold step to invest a portion of their income, but in most cases, they don’t see the returns they hoped for.

The truth is, the problem isn’t always a scam or poor planning. Sometimes, the issue lies in what we don’t see; the overlooked factors that quietly erode value, slow down growth, or distort expectations before results ever show up.

Before you ever think of investing, there are key factors you should consider that can influence your asset and affect the results you seek.

Key Factors That Can Influence Your Investment and Profit

1. Inflation

If you live in any African country, you’ve likely felt it.

You might think your investment grew by 10% this year, but if prices around you rose by 25%, you’re actually losing value. That ₦20,000 you could spend comfortably last year barely stretches to half today.

In countries like Ghana and Ethiopia, inflation has risen as high as 30% in a single year. Even Kenya, once considered relatively stable, has seen food inflation climb into double digits.

So, when you put money in an investment that grows slowly, inflation quietly outpaces it, reducing your real returns over time.

The fix is simple but powerful. Don’t just chase returns, measure real growth.

Also, choose assets or savings tools that outpace inflation rates. That could mean exploring stable foreign assets, long-term government bonds, or platforms that allow you to hold value in stronger currencies.

2. Currency Depreciation

Imagine this.

You invest $100 when the exchange rate is ₦1,500. A few months later, your return comes in, but the rate has jumped to ₦1,700. On paper, you’ve earned a profit. But in reality, the naira’s fall just erased part of your gain.

This is what happens when local currency weakness eats away at your returns. Now, it’s not just about losing to inflation. It’s about losing to exchange rates that shift faster than your balance can grow.

For many African investors, this is one of the hardest truths. You can’t control the market, but you can control exposure.

Here’s How:

  • Diversify into assets that are less affected by local volatility.

  • Keep a portion of your savings in stable currencies such as USD or EUR.

  • Use platforms like Palremit to transfer or hold funds internationally without high conversion losses or remittance fees.

That way, even when your local currency fluctuates, your investments maintain more of their value.

3. Lack of Patience

We live in a world that rewards speed. Fast returns, quick wins, instant updates. But investing is a long game, one that many people struggle to play.

A surprising number of people lose potential profit not because they invested in the wrong asset, but because they withdrew too soon. They panic when the market dips. They jump to another platform when results aren’t instant. And each time they restart, they reset their progress.

If you really want to build wealth through investing, you need to learn how to leave your emotions at the door and give it time.

Every successful investor understands that time is as important as money.

Think about it. Even the biggest companies like Apple, Dangote and MTN didn’t double overnight. So why should your small stock, fund, or savings plan?

Here’s what helps:

  • Set clear goals before investing. Is this short-term, medium-term, or long-term?

  • Study what you’re buying into. Understand its cycles. Don’t throw money at trends or pressure.

  • Track your progress quarterly, not daily.

And most importantly, stay consistent. Compounding only works when you give your investments time to breathe.

4. Not Studying the Platform

Many investors don’t lose money to bad markets, they lose it to ignorance. And that’s one of the most expensive mistakes you can make.

You hear about a new investment app or fund. You sign up, deposit funds, and wait. But you never read the details how interest is calculated, what fees apply, or what happens if you withdraw early. Over time, those hidden details quietly reduce your expected returns.

Before you invest, always know these things:

  • How does the platform calculate returns?

  • Are there withdrawal or maintenance fees?

  • Is the return fixed or market-based?

  • What’s the minimum lock period?

  • Who regulates the platform?

  • What’s their track record?

  • How easily can you access your funds if things go wrong?

Lack of clarity leads to disappointment.

So before investing, ensure that you fully understand where your money goes and how the system operates.

5. Ignoring the Ecosystem

Here’s the truth most people overlook: investments don’t exist in isolation. They live and breathe within an ecosystem shaped by policy, infrastructure, and consumer behavior.

You might invest in a local agriculture startup, but if the government suddenly cuts subsidies or fertilizer prices spike, your returns take a hit. You might buy into crypto, but a quick policy change or regulation freeze can lock your funds overnight.

That’s how fragile investing can be, not because the idea is bad, but because the environment around it shifts.

Smart investors don’t just watch charts; they study the climate their investments grow in. They understand that market movement is often a reaction to something bigger; new policies, elections, wars, or global inflation trends.

What can help you:

  • Stay informed through credible economic updates and financial reports.

  • Understand your country’s policies. Tax laws, trade restrictions, and new reforms can all affect your returns.

  • Balance local and global exposure. While it’s good to support homegrown investments, mixing in global opportunities can reduce your risk.
